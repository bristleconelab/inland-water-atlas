        How this map was made

        This atlas shows the world's lakes and the rivers between them, and for each what is measured, what is seen from space and what is only a model. Every lake of at least 10 hectares (1,427,688 of them) comes from HydroLAKES. For almost all of them, the depth and volume in that database, and in most maps and models that use it, are estimated from the lake's size and the slope of the land around it. This atlas sorts every lake by the best evidence there is for its depth:

        • Depth published: HydroLAKES took the volume from the scientific literature (266 lakes) or from the dam register GRanD (6,723 reservoirs), or a depth is recorded on Wikidata (7,616 lakes, matched when the Wikidata point lies inside the lake; deprecated values are left out, and the panel says when a depth cites no source).
        • Seen from space: 3D-LAKES traced how the shoreline moved as the water rose and fell, with ICESat-2 laser heights and Landsat images. That shows the shape of the top of the lake bed, typically a band 0.66 m high; the rest of the depth is still extrapolated.
        • Model only: neither of the above.

        What to keep in mind

        A Wikidata depth can be wrong or can belong to a nearby water body; each lake links to its item so it can be checked and corrected there. Satellite and model depths are averages over the lake, not soundings. River paths follow the mapped river network, so a lake that rarely overflows is still drawn as draining into the nearest river. GloLakes leaves out the very largest lakes, and its volumes are best read as ups and downs rather than exact amounts. River names exist only for rivers about 30 m wide or more, and a few rivers are still split or carry a tributary's name on a stretch. River flows are model averages, not gauge readings, and they do not take out what dams and irrigation use. Everything here is shared as is, to be checked against its sources before it is relied on.
